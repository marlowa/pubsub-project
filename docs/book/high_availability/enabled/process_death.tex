\subsection{A process dies while its peer is present}
\label{sec:ha:enabled:process_death}

A component dies on a machine that is otherwise healthy, and the launcher
restarts it, while a second instance of the same component is running elsewhere.
This is the case where the two configurations meet, and getting it wrong costs
more than it appears to.

\subsubsection{What is at risk}
\label{sec:ha:enabled:process_death:risk}

Not the venue's ability to trade --- the peer can act, so trading continues
whatever is decided. What is at risk is \textbf{the pair}.

If the peer promotes, the venue is served by one instance until somebody restores
the other, and it carries an unbounded exposure to a second failure for as long
as that takes. If the launcher restarts the dead instance instead, the pair is
whole again in seconds and nothing was lost.

A machine that has died cannot be restored by anything the venue does, so
promoting is the only remedy. A process that has died on a living machine can be,
so promoting discards a recovery that was available. Treating the two as one
failure is what makes a recoverable event cost resilience.

\subsubsection{What the venue must do}
\label{sec:ha:enabled:process_death:behaviour}

\begin{requirement}{R-0080}{A process death on a healthy machine does not cost the pair}
  Where a component dies and is restarted on a machine that has not failed, the
  venue shall end with two instances running, as it began.
  \rationale{The measure is whether the pair is whole again, not whether the
  venue kept trading --- which a promotion achieves on its own while leaving one
  instance down and nobody watching for the second failure.}
  \uncovered{no scenario asserts the pair is whole afterwards, only that trading continued}
\end{requirement}

\begin{requirement}{R-0081}{The peer does not promote while a restart could still succeed}
  An instance that has lost contact with the acting instance shall wait, before
  seeking to act, for at least as long as a supervised restart takes.
  \rationale{Waiting less fails the venue over for a failure that did not need
  it. Waiting much more tolerates a genuinely dead machine for longer than it
  should be. The period is therefore whatever a restart actually takes plus a
  margin, which cannot be known until restarts have been timed --- so it is
  measured rather than chosen, and it must be measured again when the components
  change.}
  \covers{ha_test:26, ha_test:27, ha_test:28}
\end{requirement}

\begin{requirement}{R-0082}{An instance that keeps failing does not disturb a healthy peer}
  A restarted instance shall not take the entitlement to act from a peer that
  holds it and is present.
  \rationale{Without this, an instance failing repeatedly takes leadership on
  each restart, dies, and hands it back --- disrupting a peer that was working
  perfectly well, once per failure. \reqref{R-0062} states the arbiter's side of
  this; this is the instance's.}
  \covers{ha_test:29, ha_test:30, ha_test:31}
\end{requirement}

\subsubsection{When the restart and the promotion race}
\label{sec:ha:enabled:process_death:race}

The two remedies run at the same time and neither is aware of the other. The
launcher is restarting the instance that died while the peer is counting down to
asking whether it may act. \textbf{The race is benign, and settles the same way
whichever ordering occurs.}

\textbf{The restart completes first.} The returning instance holds no position
and asks which it holds. No instance holds the entitlement, so it is granted it,
and resumes acting. The peer, still waiting, finds the entitlement held and
remains the follower. The entitlement did not move and the pair is whole.

\textbf{The peer asks first.} It is granted the entitlement and begins acting.
The returning instance asks, finds the position held by an instance that is
present, and takes the other one. That its configured name is primary does not
enter into it, and neither does its having held the entitlement minutes earlier.

\textbf{Both ask at once.} The claims are resolved by there being one party that
answers them. It grants one, records what it granted, and refuses the other
against that record.

In every ordering exactly one instance ends up acting, and no instance is ever
required to have detected what the other was doing --- which is the property that
makes the race benign rather than merely unlikely to go wrong.

\begin{requirement}{R-0090}{A returning instance assumes nothing and asks}
  An instance that has restarted shall hold no position until it is told which
  it holds, whatever position it held before and whatever it is configured as.
  \rationale{An instance that assumes it leads because it led before, or because
  of its configured name, becomes a second instance acting whenever the peer has
  already been promoted --- and it does so without anything having gone wrong
  with the peer.}
  \covers{ha_test:24, ha_test:25}
\end{requirement}

\begin{requirement}{R-0091}{A returning instance that finds the position vacant takes it}
  Where an instance returns and no instance holds the entitlement, it shall take
  it rather than wait.
  \rationale{A returning instance holds nothing and asks, which is what keeps a
  restart from producing a second instance acting. This is the other half of that
  rule: where the answer is that nobody holds the entitlement, the returning
  instance is the one that must take it, or a restart that outran its peer's
  promotion would leave both instances following and none acting.}
  \covers{ha_test:26, ha_test:27, ha_test:28}
\end{requirement}

\begin{requirement}{R-0092}{Simultaneous claims are resolved by one party, not by the claimants}
  Where a returning instance and its peer both seek the entitlement at once, at
  most one shall be granted it, and neither shall be required to have detected
  the other.
  \rationale{Two instances that must notice each other to stay out of each
  other's way will eventually fail to, and the moment they fail is the moment
  they are least able to communicate. Referring both claims to one party removes
  the requirement to detect anything.}
  \uncovered{no scenario times a restart to collide with a promotion}
\end{requirement}
