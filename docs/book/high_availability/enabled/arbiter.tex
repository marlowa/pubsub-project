\subsection{The arbiter and the witness}
\label{sec:ha:enabled:arbiter}

Where two instances may act and only one may act at a time, something has to
decide which. The alternative is that each decides for itself, and a pair with no
third party cannot tell ``the other is dead'' from ``I cannot see the other''.
Both answers are available to it, and both are wrong: promote on the second and
the pair ends up with two instances acting and diverging; decline on the first
and neither acts, which is the safer failure and often the later noticed,
because nothing crashes and nothing logs an error --- traffic simply stops.

\textbf{An arbiter is answerable and a consensus protocol is not necessary.} One
party decides, so only one entitlement exists at a time, and a generation number
carried on every message lets a receiver refuse an instance whose entitlement has
since passed.

\textbf{The arbitration service is not a single point of failure.} Two arbiters
run, and a witness runs alongside them so that two can reach a majority rather
than disagreeing. Losing any one of the three moves no entitlement and
interrupts nothing --- \reqref{R-0063}. The venue continues to trade even when
all of them are gone, though it is then unable to move an entitlement, which is
\cref{sec:ha:enabled:degraded}. What running a pair plus a witness achieves is
that the amount of agreement the venue needs is small, bounded, and confined to a
component that holds no venue state.

\subsubsection{What state it holds}
\label{sec:ha:enabled:arbiter:state}

Which instance leads each group, under which generation, and which instances are
currently present. It holds nothing about orders, sessions or sequence numbers,
and that is a deliberate limit rather than an omission: a component that decides
who may act must not also be a component whose own state can be wrong.

The witness holds less still. It exists to be counted, so that two arbiters can
reach a majority rather than disagreeing.

\subsubsection{Where the copy that outlives it lives}
\label{sec:ha:enabled:arbiter:durable}

Nowhere, and deliberately. Persisting the record would give a component that
holds no venue state a persistence story of its own, and a file written before a
long outage asserts an incumbent that may since have changed --- so it would need
validating against the present anyway.

\textbf{What replaces persistence is being told.} Only an instance that leads
reports itself as leading, so a report is already a claim; the generation settles
any disagreement between two of them. An arbiter that has restarted therefore
learns who leads by listening, needs no persistence, and depends on nothing that
might also have restarted. Asking the surviving arbiter is kept as an
accelerator, not as the mechanism, because the case that matters most --- both
arbiters restarted --- is the one with no peer to ask.

\subsubsection{How a restarted instance becomes current}
\label{sec:ha:enabled:arbiter:recovery}

It starts, and waits to be told. Between starting and being informed it cannot
distinguish ``nobody leads'' from ``I have not been told yet'', and the
requirements below say what it must do about that: decline, rather than guess.

\subsubsection{What the member is told}
\label{sec:ha:enabled:arbiter:member}

Nothing. The arbiter is off the path a member's orders travel, and a member
should never be able to tell that one exists. Its failures are felt only through
the components that consult it, which is why the requirements here are stated in
terms of those components rather than of members.

\begin{requirement}{R-0058}{Two instances of a group are never both entitled to act}
  At any moment, at most one instance of a group shall hold the entitlement to
  act.
  \rationale{This is the property the arbiter exists to provide, and the one
  from which the rest of this subsection follows. Two instances acting on the
  same state diverge, and nothing afterwards can say which of them was right.}
  \uncovered{no scenario asserts the property directly, only particular routes to it}
\end{requirement}

\begin{requirement}{R-0059}{An arbiter that does not know which instance is acting says so, rather than choosing one}
  Where an arbiter is asked which instance of a group may act, and it holds no
  record of that group, it shall answer that it cannot say. It shall not choose
  an instance, and it shall not treat having no record as meaning that no
  instance is currently acting.
  \rationale{An arbiter keeps nothing across its own restart. It learns which
  instance holds each entitlement by being told, so one that has just started
  holds nothing for any group until something reports to it.

  During that period two quite different situations look identical to it: a
  group in which no instance is acting, and a group whose acting instance it has
  simply not heard from yet. Choosing an instance is right in the first and
  wrong in the second, where it grants a second entitlement for a group that
  already has one --- which is the outcome the arbiter exists to prevent.

  Saying it cannot answer costs the component that asked a retry. Choosing
  wrongly costs the venue two instances acting on the same state, and nothing
  afterwards can establish which of them was right.}
  \uncovered{no scenario asks a freshly started arbiter about a group it has not been told of}
\end{requirement}

\begin{requirement}{R-0060}{Silence from an arbiter is not read as the absence of one}
  A component that asks and hears nothing shall ask again before concluding that
  no arbiter exists.
  \rationale{A silent arbiter may be one deliberately declining under
  \reqref{R-0059}. Acting on the assumption that nobody is listening, against a
  decision that was being withheld, produces exactly the second leader the
  decline was protecting.}
  \uncovered{no scenario makes an arbiter decline while a component is asking}
\end{requirement}

\begin{requirement}{R-0061}{Entitlement is held under a lease and lapses unless renewed}
  An instance's entitlement to act shall expire unless it is renewed, and the
  arbiter shall stop honouring a record whose holder is no longer present.
  \rationale{A record that is believed indefinitely is not a lease but a
  statement about something that was true once. An instance that holds its
  connection open and stops renewing is, from the outside, indistinguishable
  from a healthy one, so presence alone cannot be the test.}
  \uncovered{no scenario stops a leader renewing while it stays connected}
\end{requirement}

\begin{requirement}{R-0099}{Adjusting a machine's clock does not move an entitlement}
  The expiry of a lease shall be measured by a clock that only moves forward at a
  steady rate, and shall not be affected by the machine's notion of the time of
  day being corrected, stepped or set.
  \rationale{Time-of-day clocks are adjusted routinely --- by a time service
  correcting drift, by a leap second, by an operator. Measuring a lease against
  one means an adjustment can expire a lease that was being renewed properly, and
  move an entitlement for a reason unconnected to any component's health. An
  adjustment in the other direction is worse: a lease that should have expired
  continues to be honoured while its holder is gone.}
  \uncovered{no scenario adjusts a clock}
\end{requirement}

\begin{requirement}{R-0109}{An instance that is not making progress does not keep its entitlement}
  Holding an entitlement shall depend on the instance continuing to do the work
  the entitlement is for, and not only on its continuing to ask to keep it.
  \rationale{Renewing requires no more than a thread running. An instance
  starved of processor time, or stalled in a way that leaves its timers running,
  renews on schedule and does nothing else --- and both presence and renewal
  report it as healthy, because neither is a report of work. The venue then
  stays with an instance that has stopped, which is the failure that stops
  everything while nothing appears wrong.}
  \uncovered{no scenario starves an instance that keeps renewing}
\end{requirement}

\begin{requirement}{R-0115}{How long an entitlement lasts without renewal is a stated figure}
  The period after which an unrenewed entitlement lapses shall be a figure the
  venue states, and it shall be long enough that an instance doing its work is
  never mistaken for one that has stopped.
  \rationale{Too short and a busy instance loses its entitlement for being busy,
  which moves the venue's work to the other instance at the moment there is most
  of it. Too long and an instance that has genuinely stopped holds the
  entitlement for that period, during which nothing acts. Neither error is
  detectable from the figure alone, so it has to be derived from how long the
  work actually takes.}
  \uncovered{the period is not stated}
\end{requirement}

\begin{requirement}{R-0062}{An instance already acting keeps its entitlement when another returns}
  Where an instance holds the entitlement and is still present, an instance
  rejoining the group shall be told to follow, whatever its configured name.
  \rationale{Preferring the configured primary at this point moves the
  entitlement for no reason but a name --- and moves it to the instance that has
  just failed, away from one that is serving. The configured preference is a
  cold-start tie-break and nothing more.}
  \covers{ha_test:24, ha_test:25}
\end{requirement}

\begin{requirement}{R-0063}{Losing one arbiter, or the witness, changes nothing}
  The failure of a single arbiter, or of the witness, shall not move any
  group's entitlement and shall not interrupt the venue.
  \rationale{The arbiter is made available by redundancy rather than by being
  watched, which is what stops the question ``who watches the watcher'' from
  regressing. If losing one mattered, something would have to supervise it, and
  that thing would need supervising.}
  \covers{ha_test:2, ha_test:4, ha_test:5, ha_test:33}
\end{requirement}

\begin{requirement}{R-0064}{A generation is never issued twice, and never goes backwards}
  Each grant of entitlement shall carry a generation greater than every
  generation the venue has used. Where every instance that could have issued one
  has restarted, the new generation shall still exceed every generation
  previously used, which requires that the number outlive the processes rather
  than any one of them.
  \rationale{The generation is what lets a receiver refuse a superseded sender.
  Held only in memory it restarts at the beginning, so a pair restarted together
  agree a generation the venue has used before --- and a message left over from
  the earlier one compares as current.}
  \uncovered{no scenario restarts every instance of a group together}
\end{requirement}

\begin{requirement}{R-0065}{An instance whose entitlement has passed has what it sends refused}
  A receiver shall refuse a message carrying a generation older than the newest
  it has accepted for that group.
  \rationale{Refusing what a stale instance sends is the entire mechanism that
  keeps it from acting. It is not stopped
  and is not required to notice anything; it is simply refused at every
  interaction it attempts, which needs no hardware and no agreement from the
  instance being fenced.}
  \covers{ha_test:49}
\end{requirement}
