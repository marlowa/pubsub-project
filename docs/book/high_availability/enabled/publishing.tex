\subsection{The publisher and recorder pairs}
\label{sec:ha:enabled:publishing}

Two publishers run, and two recorders.

\subsubsection{What state it holds}
\label{sec:ha:enabled:publishing:state}

The publisher holds what it has been given, and no position on behalf of anybody:
a subscriber presents its own position when it connects
(\cref{sec:ha:disabled:mep:state}). The recorder holds one number, how far it has
published and had confirmed.

\subsubsection{Where the copy that outlives it lives}
\label{sec:ha:enabled:publishing:durable}

Both instances of a publisher receive the same records, identified by the same
sequence numbers --- the sequencer's, not numbers of the publisher's own. That
detail is what makes a pair of them useful: \textbf{a subscriber's position means
the same thing at either instance}, so moving to the survivor requires no
translation and no handover between the publishers themselves.

The recorder's position needs even less. Because it is written only after the bus
confirms, it is a lower bound on what has been published. A promoted instance
that resumes from a stale copy of it republishes some records and loses none,
which downstream absorbs by design.

\subsubsection{How a restarted instance becomes current}
\label{sec:ha:enabled:publishing:recovery}

Both are told. A publisher is told by each subscriber where it had reached; a
recorder reads its own position and presents it to a publisher. Neither needs a
handover from the instance it replaced, which is why neither pair needs the
coupling the sequencer pair has.

\subsubsection{What the member is told}
\label{sec:ha:enabled:publishing:member}

Nothing by the publisher. The recorder's failure reaches members only through
\reqref{R-0053}, and the point of running two is that it should not reach them at
all.

\begin{requirement}{R-0077}{A subscriber resumes at the surviving instance without loss}
  Where the instance a subscriber was reading from is lost, the venue shall
  supply every record after the position that subscriber presents, from the
  instance it moves to.
  \rationale{A subscriber's position is expressed in the venue's own sequence
  numbers rather than in anything a publisher assigns, so it is meaningful to
  either instance. Nothing has to be handed over.}
  \uncovered{no scenario moves a subscriber between publisher instances}
\end{requirement}

\begin{requirement}{R-0078}{A promoted recorder republishes rather than skips}
  Where publishing to the bus is taken over by another instance, the venue shall
  resume from a position no later than the last publish the bus confirmed, and
  shall publish again whatever it cannot establish was confirmed.
  \rationale{The position is a lower bound by construction, so resuming from a
  stale one costs duplicates and resuming from an optimistic one costs records.
  \reqref{R-0049} permits the first and forbids the second.}
  \uncovered{no scenario, and none is possible until the recorder exists}
\end{requirement}

\begin{requirement}{R-0079}{Losing one publisher or one recorder does not halt the venue}
  The loss of a single publisher instance, or of a single recorder instance,
  shall not stop members trading.
  \rationale{\reqref{R-0053} halts the venue when no recorder is publishing.
  Running two is what makes that condition rare, and a halt on losing one of them
  would make the pair worse than useless.}
  \uncovered{no scenario, and none is possible until the recorder exists}
\end{requirement}
