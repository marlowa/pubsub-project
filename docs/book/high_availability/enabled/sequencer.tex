\subsection{The sequencer pair}
\label{sec:ha:enabled:sequencer}

Two sequencers run. One of them holds the entitlement to act. The other receives
everything the acting instance does, and waits.

\subsubsection{What state it holds}
\label{sec:ha:enabled:sequencer:state}

The pair holds the same state as the single sequencer in
\cref{sec:ha:disabled:sequencer}: the ordering, the log, the generation and each
session's numbering. The pair holds it twice, on two machines with two disks.

The follower builds its copy from the same records, in the same order, as the
leader writes them. A session's numbering reaches the follower by the same route
it reaches the leader, because gateways report to both instances. The follower
therefore holds what a restarted instance would have to be told.

\subsubsection{Where the copy that outlives it lives}
\label{sec:ha:enabled:sequencer:durable}

The copy lives on the other machine, which is the point of this configuration. A
failed disk, a stopped machine and a hung kernel each leave the venue's record of
what it has done intact, because the venue wrote a second copy as it wrote the
first.

\textbf{The rule that makes the second machine worth running governs when the
venue acts on an order.} The leader writes the order to its log and sends the
record to the follower. The leader gives the order to the matching engine only
after the follower has acknowledged the record. Every execution report the engine
produces therefore concerns an order that both machines hold. While the follower
keeps up, a member is never told about an order that exists on one machine only.

That ordering adds a round trip to the path every order takes, and it is what
gives the venue the property the second machine is run for. Removing the round
trip to shorten the path would remove that property.

\textbf{A follower that cannot keep up costs the venue its resilience, not its
service.} A follower stops acknowledging when it has died, when its machine is
too slow to keep up, or when the link between the two machines is down. In each
case the leader carries on without it, as \cref{sec:ha:enabled:degraded} requires
of a group reduced to one instance, and reports that it is doing so. When the
follower has caught up, the leader goes back to waiting for its acknowledgements.

Before the leader gives the matching engine an order the follower does not hold,
it records a statement that the follower may not lead. The leader writes the
statement to its own disk and sends it to the follower and to the arbiter. The
leader acts on the order only after one of those two has recorded the statement
and confirmed it. From then on, every combination of voters that could make the
follower leader includes a voter that refuses to.

While the leader carries on alone, a member can be told about an order that only
the leader's machine holds. That is the price of continuing to trade. If the
leader's process stops and is restarted, it still holds those orders, takes the
lead again and sends the follower what it lacks. If the leader's machine is lost
for good, the orders only that machine held are lost with it, and members may
hold execution reports for them. The follower does not take the lead in either
case, so trading in the group stops until the old leader returns, or until an
operator decides to let the follower lead and the venue tells members which of
their orders were lost.

\subsubsection{How a restarted instance becomes current}
\label{sec:ha:enabled:sequencer:recovery}

A promoted follower is already current, or close to it. It holds the records and
has acknowledged them, and it needs only to apply what it has received and not
yet applied.

\textbf{A promotion recovers more than the restart in
\cref{sec:ha:disabled:sequencer} does.} A restarted sequencer recovers its
sequence number from its log, its generation from a file, and its sessions by
being told, which leaves it holding nothing for a session that was not bound at
the time. A promoted follower was told the same things as they happened, so it
holds the numbering of sessions that no gateway would report.

Leadership is decided by leases, not by comparing shared generations. Each
instance asks its peer and the active arbiter for a lease. Its own vote and an
unexpired grant from either voter form a majority, which permits it to act. The
arbiter is a voter in every election, not only when the sequencers cannot see
each other.

\subsubsection{What the member is told}
\label{sec:ha:enabled:sequencer:member}

Nothing. The member sees a brief pause in responses and no break in numbering.

\begin{requirement}{R-0104}{A member is told nothing false while an entitlement is moving}
  Between the loss of the instance that was acting and the moment its replacement
  has caught up, a member may receive no answer to what it sends, and shall not
  receive an answer that is wrong. An order shall not be rejected as unknown, and
  an order shall not be reported accepted, on the basis of state that is not yet
  current.
  \rationale{The interval is short, but it is not zero, and a member cannot tell that it is in
  one. Silence during the interval is a delay, and a member is built to tolerate a
  delay. An answer given from state that is still being assembled is a statement
  the venue cannot support, and the member acts on that statement.}
  \uncovered{no scenario sends orders during a failover and checks the answers}
\end{requirement}

\begin{requirement}{R-0066}{While the follower keeps up, a member is never told of an order held on one machine only}
  While the second instance is keeping up, an execution report shall not be sent
  to a member until the record of the order it concerns has been acknowledged by
  the second instance.
  \rationale{An order that exists in one place is lost when that place is lost, so telling a
  member about such an order commits the venue to something it may not be able to
  honour. This is the property the second machine is run for, and the other
  requirements in this section depend on it. The requirement applies only while
  the second instance keeps up, because the leader carries on without a second
  instance that does not, under \reqref{R-0156}.}
  \uncovered{no scenario asserts that a report waits for the follower}
\end{requirement}

\begin{requirement}{R-0067}{Losing a machine does not lose a committed order}
  An order committed before a machine was lost shall be processed and answered by
  the surviving instance.
  \rationale{Committing the order is the venue taking it. Losing one of the two machines
  holding that order is the failure the pair exists to absorb.}
  \uncovered{no scenario asserts an order committed before the kill is answered after it}
\end{requirement}

\begin{requirement}{R-0068}{The venue keeps trading when the acting sequencer is lost}
  Members shall continue to be able to place orders across the loss of the
  sequencer instance that was acting, without reconnecting and without being
  told to retry.
  \rationale{This is the most visible reason for running two machines, and a member would
  notice immediately if it did not hold. It is stated separately from the
  requirements about what survives because continuing to trade and keeping what was
  taken are different properties. A venue can meet this requirement while failing
  every other requirement in this section.}
  \covers{ha_test:1}
\end{requirement}

\begin{requirement}{R-0069}{A member's session survives a failover}
  A member's numbering, and the orders open under its session, shall be
  unaffected by leadership moving between sequencers.
  \rationale{A failover the member can detect has not achieved what it was for. The venue
  tells the follower each session's position as that position advances, for this
  reason.}
  \uncovered{no scenario fails the sequencer over under a live session}
\end{requirement}

\begin{requirement}{R-0156}{A follower that cannot keep up is treated as a lost instance}
  Where the second instance has acknowledged nothing for 100 milliseconds while
  records are waiting for it, or where the leader's storage for orders waiting on
  it is full, the leader shall carry on without it, as \reqref{R-0093} requires for
  a group that has lost an instance.
  \rationale{A follower that is running but behind protects nothing while it is behind, in the
  same way as a follower that has died. If the venue stopped for a slow follower
  and carried on for a dead one, a follower that was unwell would do more harm
  than one that had stopped. A fault in the second instance reduces the venue's
  resilience, and the venue goes on trading.}
  \covers{ha_test:60}
\end{requirement}

\begin{requirement}{R-0157}{A leader acts on an order its follower lacks only once a second voter has recorded that the follower may not lead}
  Before a leader gives the matching engine an order whose record the second
  instance has not acknowledged, the leader shall record on its own disk that the
  second instance may not lead, and one of its other two voters shall have
  recorded that statement and confirmed it.
  \rationale{The follower then lacks orders that the matching engine has acted on and that
  members may have been told of. If the follower led, it would number new orders
  from a point below orders the engine has already applied, and those orders would
  be missing from the venue's record of the day. The follower can become leader
  only with a vote from the old leader or from the arbiter. The old leader refuses,
  because the statement is on its disk. Either the arbiter has recorded the
  statement and refuses, or the follower has recorded it and does not ask.}
  \covers{ha_test:63, ha_test:64}
\end{requirement}

\begin{requirement}{R-0158}{The venue reports when a leader starts and stops carrying on without its follower}
  A leader shall report when it starts to carry on without the second instance,
  and again when it stops, saying why, in a form an operator can raise an alert on.
  \rationale{A member cannot see that the venue is running on one machine, so nobody outside
  the venue learns of it unless the venue says so. The time spent running on one
  machine is the venue's exposure to losing the leader's machine, which
  \reqref{R-0094} requires to be knowable. When the follower is connected and
  behind, the sequencer writes a warning to its log and sets the gauge
  \texttt{sequencer\_running\_alone} to 1, and logs again and sets the gauge to 0
  when the follower has caught up.}
  \covers{ha_test:60}
\end{requirement}

\begin{gap}{BUG-0221}
  The gauge reads 0 while the leader carries on with no follower connected. The
  sequencer logs a warning that the connection to its peer was lost, and an alert
  on the gauge alone misses a follower that has died.
\end{gap}

\begin{requirement}{R-0159}{A leader waits for its follower again once the follower has caught up}
  When the second instance has acknowledged every record the leader has written,
  the leader shall again wait for its acknowledgements before acting on an order,
  and shall then withdraw the statement that the second instance may not lead.
  \rationale{Carrying on without the follower is a response to the follower falling behind,
  and it ends when the follower has caught up. From that moment the protection of
  \reqref{R-0066} is available again, and the venue takes it. The leader goes back to
  waiting before it withdraws the statement, so that the follower is never
  permitted to lead while it lacks an order the engine has acted on.}
  \covers{ha_test:60, ha_test:63}
\end{requirement}

\begin{requirement}{R-0160}{Trading in the group halts if the leader is lost while carrying on without its follower}
  Where the leader is lost while it is carrying on without the second instance,
  the second instance shall not take the lead, and trading in the group shall
  resume when the old leader returns.
  \rationale{The only complete record of the orders the engine has acted on is on the old
  leader's disk. If the follower took the lead, those orders would be lost, though
  members may hold execution reports for them, and the follower would number new
  orders over them. Waiting for the old leader gives the same outcome as losing a
  leader that had no follower at all. A restarted leader reads its log, takes the
  lead and sends the follower what it lacks.}
  \covers{ha_test:63, ha_test:64}
\end{requirement}

\begin{requirement}{R-0161}{An operator can let the follower lead when the old leader's machine is lost for good}
  Where the old leader's machine is lost for good while the leader was carrying
  on without the second instance, an operator shall be able to let the second
  instance lead, and the venue shall log at Warning what was done and why.
  \rationale{Nothing in the venue can recover the orders that only the lost machine held, so
  without this the group cannot trade again until that machine returns. The venue
  cannot tell a machine that is lost for good from one that is slow to restart.
  Only the operator knows which it is, so only the operator can accept the loss of
  those orders.}
  \uncovered{not built: nothing clears the statement at the arbiter and at the follower}
\end{requirement}

\begin{requirement}{R-0162}{Members are told which of their orders were lost with the old leader's machine}
  After an operator has let the second instance lead under \reqref{R-0161}, the
  venue shall establish which orders members were sent execution reports for that
  the surviving instance's log does not hold, and each member affected shall be
  told which of its orders these are.
  \rationale{A member holding a report for an order the venue no longer has will act on that
  report. It may hedge a fill the venue has no record of, or try to cancel an order
  the venue does not hold. The venue cannot undo what the member was told. It can
  tell the member what was lost, so that the member acts on accurate information.}
  \uncovered{not built: no procedure compares what members were told with the surviving log}
\end{requirement}

\begin{gap}{BUG-0222}
  No operator tool lets the follower lead after the old leader's machine is lost
  for good, and no procedure tells members which of their orders were lost with
  it. A group whose leader's machine is lost while the leader is carrying on
  without its follower stays halted until that machine returns.
\end{gap}

\begin{requirement}{R-0100}{Waiting for a disk or for a peer does not stop a component working}
  A component shall continue to process what arrives while it is waiting for a
  write to complete or for a second instance to acknowledge one, and shall
  continue to renew its entitlement and to answer enquiries about its health
  throughout.
  \rationale{The requirements in this section make a component wait for a disk and for a peer,
  and both can be slow for reasons unconnected to the venue. A component that stops
  processing while it waits also stops answering members, stops replying to its
  peer and stops renewing its lease. From outside, such a component looks the same
  as one that has stopped, so the venue restarts it, and a slow disk becomes a
  restart rather than a delay.}
  \uncovered{no scenario makes a disk or a peer slow}
\end{requirement}

\begin{requirement}{R-0110}{How far behind a second instance may fall is a stated figure}
  The point at which a second instance is too far behind shall be a figure the
  venue states, expressed in what has not been acknowledged rather than in
  elapsed time alone.
  \rationale{The leader carries on without the second instance once that instance is too far
  behind, under \reqref{R-0156}. Neither implementing nor testing that requirement is
  possible without knowing when the point is reached. A figure expressed only as a
  duration moves with the rate orders arrive, so a venue under load reaches it
  after far fewer records than a quiet venue does. The sequencer's figure is 16,384
  orders waiting on the second instance, set by \texttt{orders\_held\_at\_most}.}
  \uncovered{no scenario fills the storage for orders waiting on the second instance}
\end{requirement}

\begin{requirement}{R-0071}{A sequencer that finds its own record damaged stops rather than serving from it}
  An instance that cannot read its log whole shall not act on the part it can
  read, and the instance holding an undamaged copy shall act instead.
  \rationale{This is the answer that \cref{sec:ha:disabled:recovery} states the disabled
  configuration does not have. An instance serving from a partial log assigns
  numbers it has assigned before, and answers orders other than the ones it
  committed.}
  \uncovered{no scenario damages a log}
\end{requirement}

\begin{requirement}{R-0072}{A pair that can see each other settles leadership between themselves}
  Where both instances are present and neither holds the entitlement, they shall
  reach the same answer without referring it, and shall reach it whichever
  started first.
  \rationale{A peer's grant can provide the second vote needed for a majority, so the pair can
  decide leadership when the active arbiter is unavailable. The implementation also asks the
  arbiter, whose grant can provide that vote when the peer cannot.}
  \covers{ha_test:36, ha_test:37}
\end{requirement}
