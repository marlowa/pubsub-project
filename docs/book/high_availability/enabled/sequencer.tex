\subsection{The sequencer pair}
\label{sec:ha:enabled:sequencer}

Two sequencers run. One holds the entitlement to act; the other receives
everything it does and waits.

\subsubsection{What state it holds}
\label{sec:ha:enabled:sequencer:state}

The same state as in \cref{sec:ha:disabled:sequencer} --- the ordering, the log,
the generation, each session's numbering --- but now held twice, on two machines
with two disks.

The follower's copy is not a backup taken periodically. It is built from the same
records, in the same order, as they are written. And a session's numbering
reaches the follower by the same route it reaches the leader: gateways report to
both instances, so the follower holds what a restarted instance would have to be
told.

\subsubsection{Where the copy that outlives it lives}
\label{sec:ha:enabled:sequencer:durable}

On the other machine, and this is the point of the configuration. A disk that
fails, a machine that stops, a kernel that hangs --- none of these takes the
venue's record of what it has done, because a second copy was written as the
first was.

\textbf{The rule that makes it worth having is about when a member is told.} An
order becomes durable in two stages: the leader writes it, which permits the
order to be given to the matching engine; and the follower acknowledges it, which
permits the resulting execution report to be given to the gateway. A member is
therefore never told about an order whose existence rests on one machine.

That ordering costs a round trip on the reporting path and buys the property the
whole configuration exists for. It is not an optimisation to be revisited when
the path is measured.

\subsubsection{How a restarted instance becomes current}
\label{sec:ha:enabled:sequencer:recovery}

A promoted follower is already current, or nearly: it holds the records and has
acknowledged them, and needs only to apply anything it has received and not yet
applied.

\textbf{Here the comparison with \cref{sec:ha:disabled:sequencer} favours the
pair, and by more than it does elsewhere.} A restarted sequencer recovers its
sequence number from its log, its generation from a file, and its sessions by
being told --- which leaves it holding nothing for a session that was not bound
at the time. A promoted follower was told the same things as they happened, so it
holds the sessions nobody would think to report. The two operations recover
different amounts, and this is the one that recovers more.

The pair also settles leadership between themselves when they can see each other,
rather than referring it. Both hold both instance identities and both
generations, so both compute the same answer from the same inputs. The arbiter is
for the case a pair cannot settle alone --- when one cannot see the other, and a
second claimant might exist unseen.

\subsubsection{What the member is told}
\label{sec:ha:enabled:sequencer:member}

Ideally nothing at all: a brief pause in responses, and no break in numbering.

\begin{requirement}{R-0066}{A member is never told of an order held on one machine only}
  An execution report shall not be sent to a member until the record of the order
  it concerns has been acknowledged by a second instance.
  \rationale{Telling a member about an order that exists in one place is a
  promise the venue cannot keep if that place is lost. This is the property two
  machines are being run for, and every other requirement here is subordinate to
  it.}
  \uncovered{no scenario asserts that a report waits for the follower}
\end{requirement}

\begin{requirement}{R-0067}{Losing a machine does not lose a committed order}
  An order committed before a machine was lost shall be processed and answered by
  the surviving instance.
  \rationale{Commitment is the venue taking the order, and the loss of one of two
  machines holding it is the failure the pair exists to absorb.}
  \uncovered{no scenario asserts an order committed before the kill is answered after it}
\end{requirement}

\begin{requirement}{R-0068}{The venue keeps trading when the acting sequencer is lost}
  Members shall continue to be able to place orders across the loss of the
  instance that was acting.
  \covers{ha_test:1}
\end{requirement}

\begin{requirement}{R-0069}{A member's session survives a failover}
  A member's numbering, and the orders open under its session, shall be
  unaffected by leadership moving between sequencers.
  \rationale{A failover the member can detect is a failover that has failed at
  its purpose. The follower is told each session's position as it advances, for
  exactly this reason.}
  \uncovered{no scenario fails the sequencer over under a live session}
\end{requirement}

\begin{requirement}{R-0070}{A follower that falls behind stops the venue confirming, rather than being dropped}
  Where the second instance cannot keep up, the venue shall stop sending
  execution reports rather than continue without it.
  \rationale{Dropping the follower to keep the reporting path moving abandons
  \reqref{R-0066} silently and at exactly the moment it matters. Members see
  orders received and no reports, which is visible and recoverable; the
  alternative is invisible.}
  \uncovered{no scenario makes the follower lag}
\end{requirement}

\begin{requirement}{R-0071}{A sequencer that finds its own record damaged stops rather than serving from it}
  An instance that cannot read its log whole shall not act on the part it can
  read, and the instance holding an undamaged copy shall act instead.
  \rationale{This is the answer \cref{sec:ha:disabled:recovery} says the disabled
  configuration does not have. Serving from a partial log assigns numbers that
  have been assigned before and answers orders that were not the ones committed.}
  \uncovered{no scenario damages a log}
\end{requirement}

\begin{requirement}{R-0072}{A pair that can see each other settles leadership between themselves}
  Where both instances are present and neither holds the entitlement, they shall
  reach the same answer without referring it, and shall reach it whichever
  started first.
  \rationale{Both hold both identities and both generations, so both compute the
  same result from the same inputs. Referring it instead makes the venue wait on
  a component that, having just started itself, is the least informed party
  present.}
  \covers{ha_test:36, ha_test:37}
\end{requirement}
