\subsection{The member's own application}
\label{sec:ha:disabled:client}

A member's application dies and is restarted. This is the only failure in this
section that is not the venue's, and the venue's obligations are correspondingly
different: it has nothing to recover, and everything to hold.

It is also the failure the venue is most often subjected to. A member restarts
its systems on its own schedule, for its own reasons, without telling anybody.

\subsubsection{What state it holds}
\label{sec:ha:disabled:client:state}

Its own view: which orders it believes are live at the venue, the identifiers it
has used, and where its side of the conversation had reached. None of this is
the venue's to keep, and the venue cannot assume any of it survived.

What the venue holds \emph{on the member's behalf} is a different list, and it
is the subject of the rest of this subsection: the session's numbering, the
orders open under it, and the terms the member was admitted under.

\subsubsection{Where the copy that outlives it lives}
\label{sec:ha:disabled:client:durable}

Wherever the member chose to put it, which the venue cannot know and must not
depend on. A member may return knowing exactly where it was, or knowing nothing
at all, and both are legitimate.

The consequence is that the venue must be able to answer rather than to assume.
A returning member that has kept its numbering resumes; one that has lost it
says so and starts afresh; and either way it must be able to establish what the
venue is holding for it --- which it currently cannot, as recorded at
\cref{sec:ha:disabled:gateway:member}.

\subsubsection{How a restarted instance becomes current}
\label{sec:ha:disabled:client:recovery}

It connects, logs on, and either resumes its numbering or asks to restart it. The
venue supplies what it remembers of the session, and the member reconciles its own
view against what the venue reports.

\subsubsection{What the member is told}
\label{sec:ha:disabled:client:member}

\begin{requirement}{R-0054}{A member that returns in time finds its orders untouched}
  Where a member's connection ends unexpectedly and the same comp id logs on
  again within the period provisioned for it, none of that member's orders shall
  have been cancelled.
  \rationale{The period exists so that a member's own restart is a reconnection
  rather than an event. Cancelling and then having the member replace the orders
  costs it its place and tells the market something that did not happen.}
  \covers{ha_test:19}
\end{requirement}

\begin{requirement}{R-0055}{A member that does not return has its orders cancelled as it asked, and is told when it comes back}
  Where the period expires without the member returning, the orders it asked to
  have cancelled shall be cancelled, and the reports of those cancellations
  shall be delivered to it when it next connects.
  \rationale{The member configured this to bound its exposure while it is
  absent. Cancelling without recording it for the member's return leaves it to
  discover the change by inference.}
  \uncovered{no scenario lets the period expire and then reconnects}
\end{requirement}

\begin{requirement}{R-0056}{An order meant to outlive the session is not cancelled by a disconnection}
  An order whose time in force says it survives the trading session shall not be
  cancelled because the member's connection ended.
  \rationale{Such an order is by definition intended to outlive the session that
  placed it. Cancelling it because a socket closed defeats what the member
  asked for, and it is the one instruction a member cannot restate cheaply.}
  \uncovered{no scenario places an order that outlives the session and then drops it}
\end{requirement}

\begin{requirement}{R-0057}{A member that has lost its own numbering can start afresh without losing its orders}
  A member that asks to restart its numbering shall be given a session numbered
  from the beginning, and the orders it holds at the venue shall be unaffected.
  \rationale{Losing a sequence store is an ordinary consequence of a member's
  own restart, and it says nothing about its orders. Tying the two together
  would make a member choose between a session it can use and a book it can
  keep.}
  \uncovered{no scenario restarts a member's numbering with orders open}
\end{requirement}
