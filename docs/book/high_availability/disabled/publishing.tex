\subsection{The matching engine publisher}
\label{sec:ha:disabled:mep}

The publisher takes what the venue has done and makes it available to consumers
inside the venue --- market data, and the recorder of \cref{sec:ha:disabled:oar}.
It dies and is restarted. No member notices directly; what stops is anything
downstream hearing about what the venue is doing.

\subsubsection{What state it holds}
\label{sec:ha:disabled:mep:state}

The record of what it has been given, and how far each subscriber has consumed
it. The second of those it does not have to remember: a subscriber presents its
own position when it reconnects, so the publisher is told rather than obliged to
recall. That is a deliberate and useful property, and it is why this subsection
is the shortest in the section.

\subsubsection{Where the copy that outlives it lives}
\label{sec:ha:disabled:mep:durable}

Its own log, on disk. What it may discard from that log is bounded by the
slowest subscriber that has not yet consumed it.

\begin{gap}{BUG-0048}
  What the publisher discards is anchored to a periodic mark rather than to the
  positions its subscribers have reached. A subscriber that returns having been
  away may therefore find that what it needed is gone.
\end{gap}

\subsubsection{How a restarted instance becomes current}
\label{sec:ha:disabled:mep:recovery}

It reopens its log and waits to be told where each returning subscriber has
reached. As with \cref{sec:ha:disabled:gateway} there is one such operation
rather than two.

\subsubsection{What the member is told}
\label{sec:ha:disabled:mep:member}

Nothing: a member is not a subscriber. The requirements here protect the
consumers.

\begin{requirement}{R-0047}{A subscriber resumes where it had reached, across a restart of the publisher}
  The venue shall supply a returning subscriber with every record after the
  position it presents, and shall not require it to have been present
  throughout.
  \rationale{A subscriber that must be continuously connected to avoid missing
  records cannot be restarted, upgraded or briefly partitioned --- which is to
  say it cannot be operated.}
  \uncovered{no scenario restarts the publisher under a connected subscriber}
\end{requirement}

\begin{requirement}{R-0048}{A record is retained until every subscriber has consumed it}
  The venue shall not discard a record that any subscriber entitled to it has
  not yet consumed.
  \rationale{Where records are discarded on a timer rather than when they have
  been consumed, a subscriber that was briefly away loses records without
  anything reporting it. The loss is found by the subscriber, not by the
  publisher that caused it.}
  \uncovered{no scenario returns a subscriber after its records were discarded}
\end{requirement}

\subsection{The order activity recorder}
\label{sec:ha:disabled:oar}

The recorder subscribes to the publisher and forwards what the venue has done to
an enterprise bus, which is how systems outside the venue learn of it. Unlike
every other component in this section, its failure is felt outside the venue
entirely.

\subsubsection{What state it holds}
\label{sec:ha:disabled:oar:state}

One number: how far it has published and had confirmed. Everything else it can
obtain again from the publisher.

\subsubsection{Where the copy that outlives it lives}
\label{sec:ha:disabled:oar:durable}

Its own record of that number, written to disk --- and \textbf{written only
after the bus has confirmed the publish, never before}. That ordering is the
whole of the durability design. A failure between publishing and recording the
position republishes, which downstream absorbs. A failure the other way skips a
record permanently, and nothing afterwards can tell.

It follows that the recorded position need only be a lower bound, so writing it
lazily is safe however stale it becomes: staleness costs duplicate volume and
nothing else.

\subsubsection{How a restarted instance becomes current}
\label{sec:ha:disabled:oar:recovery}

It reads its position, presents it to the publisher, and continues. Records it
had already published but not recorded are published again. This is expected
rather than tolerated: an enterprise bus consumer is required to cope with
replay, and the alternative is a design that loses.

\subsubsection{What the member is told}
\label{sec:ha:disabled:oar:member}

Nothing directly, until the last requirement below, which stops the market.

\begin{requirement}{R-0049}{Nothing is lost from the external record, though it may appear more than once}
  Every record the venue produces shall reach the enterprise bus at least once.
  Duplicates are permitted; omissions are not.
  \rationale{Loss outside the venue cannot be repaired by the venue, because
  nothing there knows what is missing. Duplication can be repaired by anybody
  holding the record's identity.}
  \uncovered{no scenario, and none is possible until the recorder exists}
\end{requirement}

\begin{requirement}{R-0050}{Every externally published record carries an identity a consumer can deduplicate on}
  Each record shall carry a stable producer identity and a sequence number
  assigned by the venue.
  \rationale{This is what makes \reqref{R-0049} usable rather than merely true.
  Without it a consumer cannot tell a duplicate from a second event, so
  permitting duplicates would mean permitting the record to be wrong.}
  \uncovered{no scenario, and none is possible until the recorder exists}
\end{requirement}

\begin{requirement}{R-0051}{The recorder never resumes beyond its last confirmed publish}
  The position the recorder resumes from shall never be later than the last
  publish the bus confirmed.
  \rationale{Recording a position before the publish is confirmed converts a
  crash into permanent omission, which \reqref{R-0049} forbids. The ordering is
  not an optimisation and may not be relaxed for throughput.}
  \uncovered{no scenario, and none is possible until the recorder exists}
\end{requirement}

\begin{requirement}{R-0052}{A restart of the recorder does not stop trading}
  The venue shall continue to accept orders while the recorder restarts and
  catches up.
  \rationale{A supervised restart is a routine event the venue recovers from
  unaided. Stopping the market for it would make the recorder's availability,
  rather than the venue's, the thing that decides whether members can trade.}
  \uncovered{no scenario, and none is possible until the recorder exists}
\end{requirement}

\begin{requirement}{R-0053}{Where external publishing cannot resume, trading is halted}
  Where no recorder is publishing and the unpublished backlog has passed a
  stated bound, the venue shall halt trading, and shall say that it has.
  \rationale{Trading that outruns the record of it by more than a little cannot
  be reconstructed afterwards by anyone. Unlike conditions the venue cannot
  detect, this one is plain --- no recorder is connected, and the backlog is
  counted --- so it can be acted on without a person present. The bound must
  exceed what a supervised restart and catch-up take, or \reqref{R-0052} is
  contradicted; it therefore has to be measured rather than chosen.}
  \uncovered{no scenario, and none is possible until the recorder exists}
\end{requirement}
